# The harmonic first-rank gate pays every relative rank

## Theorem

Let G be the tangent cone of a genuine numerical semigroup over an arbitrary field k, with multiplicity m, embedding dimension e, and defect d=m-e. Let x=[m], A=G/xG, T=ann_G x. Let E consist of r distinct actual non-x variables, with r>=3, such that A/(E)A is square-zero. Suppose

    d>=5,
    2e+d >= C(2r,r)+r^2-r+1.                              (T)

Then EVERY actual relative generic rank is paid:

    c_j^{k[x,E]}(G) <= R_j(e,d,r),  1<=j<=r.

Here R_j is the actual ordinary-target coefficient from R54. For an actual ordinary interval target, the inherited R54 comparison therefore proves every P14 Betti row. This is an infinite sufficient chamber for arbitrary r; it is not unrestricted global P14.

The proof uses all of the actual induced d2 ranks with their correct negative sign. It remains valid where the higher-cut homology has characteristic-dependent ranks.

## Named dependencies and novelty boundary

The numerical/tower inputs are the R55 actual N/Q opposite-jump filtration and its exact truncation sequences, and the new R56 weighted jump-volume identity proved in `../tower/WEIGHTED_JUMP_VOLUME_AND_R3_FIRST_RANK.md`:

    S=sum_(s=2)^nu length(X_s) <= d V,
    V=e+d/2-3/2,
    X_s=(A/F_(s-1))_(>=s),
    sum_(s=2)^nu q_s=d,  mu(X_s)=q_s.

The harmonic monomial-border argument is proved in the border branch and recalled below.

There is one stronger explicitly named external input: **Peeva's consecutive-cancellation theorem for a homogeneous ideal and its same-Hilbert-function lex ideal**. We use this theorem over the chosen field. It is stronger than coefficientwise Betti domination and is not claimed as a new result here. Primary verification: Giulio Caviglia and Manoj Kummini, *Poset embeddings of Hilbert functions and Betti numbers*, Introduction p2 (Peeva04 Theorem1.1), Proposition3.9 and Lemma3.10 pp6–7; author-hosted PDF https://www.math.purdue.edu/~gcavigli/Articles-PDF/%5BM13%5D.pdf . The paper works over a field without imposing characteristic zero for this cancellation result. Its reference is I. Peeva, *Consecutive cancellations in Betti numbers*, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc.132 (2004), no.12,3503–3507. The official Peeva publisher PDF was not directly retrieved in this run; the primary Caviglia–Kummini paper explicitly states and develops the cancellation theorem. Eliahou–Kervaire for stable ideals is already an inherited named input.

The new work here is the generic-rank consequence of cancellation, its all-rank harmonic transfer through the actual tower, and the positive-binomial target comparison. No alternating inequality is inferred from Betti domination alone.

## 1. Harmonic bounds for every finite generic rank

Put

    alpha=(r-1)/(r+1),
    B=Cat_r-1,  Cat_r=C(2r,r)/(r+1).

For a finite P=k[E]-module X generated by q elements in one degree, we claim

    c_j^P(X) <= C(r-2,j-1)[alpha*length(X)+B*q],
                 1<=j<=r-1.                              (1)

Take a homogeneous initial submodule of the kernel of a minimal presentation P(-a)^q -> X. Its quotient is a direct sum of q shifted cyclic Artin quotients P/I_i, with total length length(X).

Generic ranks do not decrease in this degeneration. To see this directly, fix an internal degree. The Koszul chain dimensions n_i are determined by the Hilbert function, so they remain fixed. Telescoping the chain-rank formula gives

    c_j= sum_(i=0)^j (-1)^(j-i)n_i - rank(partial_(j+1)).

Specialization cannot increase the boundary rank. Thus it cannot decrease c_j, degree by degree and then after summation.

For each cyclic quotient replace I_i by its lex ideal L_i with the same Hilbert function. Peeva's named theorem obtains the former graded Betti table from the latter by consecutive cancellations. Canceling one pair in homological positions s,s+1 decreases precisely c_s by one and leaves every other generic rank unchanged. Therefore

    c_j(P/I_i)<=c_j(P/L_i).

Let L be a finite lex ideal and let max(u) denote the largest variable index appearing in a minimal generator u. Eliahou–Kervaire and alternating the binomial coefficients give, exactly for j>=1,

    c_j(P/L)=sum_(u in G(L), max(u)>=2) C(max(u)-2,j-1).    (2)

Indeed the unique generator having max(u)=1 cancels the beta0 contribution; every other generator contributes the displayed partial alternating binomial sum. In particular,

    c_j(P/L)<=C(r-2,j-1)[mu(L)-1].                         (3)

For completeness the harmonic border bound is

    mu(L)-1 <= r-1+sum_(h>=1) [(r-1)/(h+1)] H_(P/L)(h).

It follows from normalized matching between monomials of successive degrees: choose a degree h+1 monomial uniformly and remove variable i with probability exponent_i/(h+1). The lower monomial is uniform, and every predecessor of a minimal border word is standard. Telescoping the resulting border bounds gives the displayed inequality. For h>=r the coefficient is at most alpha; for h<r use H(h)<=C(r+h-1,h). The exact finite sum is

    (r-1)-alpha
      +sum_(h=1)^(r-1) [((r-1)/(h+1))-alpha] C(r+h-1,h)
       =Cat_r-1=B.

Consequently mu(L)-1<=alpha*length(P/L)+B. Combining with (3), and summing the q cyclic pieces, proves (1). Extend the notation to j=r by C(r-2,r-1)=0 and c_r^P(X)=0.

## 2. The actual tower pays higher ranks jointly

For the actual jump tower put

    M_s=(A/F_(s-1))_(>=s+1),
    K_s=F_s/F_(s-1).

There are actual short exact sequences

    0 -> K_s -> M_s -> X_(s+1) -> 0,

with X_(nu+1)=0. The opposite-jump filtration of T has shifted strata K_s. The generic-rank long exact sequence gives

    c_h(K_s)<=c_h(M_s)+c_(h+1)(X_(s+1)),
    c_h(T)<=sum_s c_h(K_s).

Moreover M_s is generated in degree s+1, with

    mu(M_s)<=r q_s,
    length(M_s)=length(X_s)-q_s.

Thus sum length(M_s)=S-d.

The low-degree quotient A/A_(>=2) is

    P/P_+^2 direct_sum k(-1)^(e-r-1).

Its exact j-th generic rank is

    L_j=(e-1)C(r-1,j)-C(r-1,j+1).                          (4)

This follows directly from the stable quadratic ideal P_+^2 and the Koszul ranks of k. In particular L_1=(r-1)(e-r/2).

Write c_j(G) for the relative rank over k[x,E]. The exact x-first identity is

    c_j(G)=c_j^P(A)+c_(j-1)^P(T)-rho_(j+1),

where rho_(j+1) is the actual induced rank. Discarding only its nonnegative value and applying extension subadditivity yields, for 2<=j<=r,

    c_j(G)<=L_j+sum_s c_j(X_s)+sum_s c_(j-1)(M_s).         (5)

Let

    p=C(r-2,j-1),  q=C(r-2,j-2).

Apply (1) to each term of (5), including its j=r zero convention. Since p+q=C(r-1,j-1),

    c_j(G)<=L_j+alpha*(p+q)S
                    +d{B(p+r q)-alpha q}.                 (6)

This is a source-specific bound on the actual tower, not a replacement of T by N or a splitting of their filtrations.

## 3. Convert the first gate into every higher budget

Condition (T), written in terms of V, is

    V>=W_r:=(r+1)(Cat_r+r-2)/2.

It implies

    B<=2V/(r+1).

Use S<=dV in (6), and discard the nonpositive term -d alpha q. Then

    c_j(G)<=L_j+dV[p+(3-4/(r+1))q]
             <=L_j+dV(p+3q).                              (7)

This is the crucial all-rank consequence of the same harmonic first gate.

## 4. Positive-binomial expansion of the actual target

Set n=d+r-1. The exact target is

    R_j=C(n,j)[e+(jd-r)/(j+1)].                            (8)

It obeys the identity

    R_j-L_j=V[C(n,j)-C(r-1,j)]+Gamma_j(d),                 (9)

where the remainder has the following manifestly nonnegative expansion for integer d>=0:

    Gamma_j(d)
      =d C(r-1,j-1)
       +1/2 sum_(t=2)^j
          [3 C(r-1,j-t)+(t-2)C(r,j-t+1)] C(d,t)
       +(j-1)/2 C(d,j+1).                                (10)

The sum is empty when j=1. To verify (10), start with

    Gamma_j(d)= [(j-1)d+3j+3-2r]/[2(j+1)] C(n,j)
                    +(d-1)C(r-1,j)/2+C(r-1,j+1),

expand C(n,j)=sum_t C(d,t)C(r-1,j-t), and use

    d C(d,t)=t C(d,t)+(t+1)C(d,t+1).

The constant binomial-basis coefficient is zero; the t=1 coefficient is C(r-1,j-1); the remaining coefficients are those displayed in (10). This proves Gamma_j(d)>=0 without any parameter testing.

Pascal and Vandermonde give, for 2<=j<=r,

    C(n,j)-C(r-1,j)
       =sum_(l=1)^d C(r-2+l,j-1)
       >=d C(r-2,j-1)+[d(d+1)/2] C(r-2,j-2)
       =d p+[d(d+1)/2]q.

The hypothesis d>=5 therefore implies

    C(n,j)-C(r-1,j)>=d(p+3q).

Since V>0, equations (9)-(10) now give

    R_j>=L_j+dV(p+3q)>=c_j(G).

This pays every j>=2, including the top rank j=r, using the same gate as the first rank.

## 5. The first rank and the resulting all-row theorem

The same actual tower, using mu(M_s)<=r q_s rather than a higher-rank bound, gives

    c_1(G)<=L_1+r d+sum_s c_1(X_s)
             <=L_1+(r+B)d+alpha S
             <=L_1+d(r+B+alpha V).

At j=1 the exact identity (9) reads R_1-L_1=d(V+1). Thus c_1<=R_1 follows from

    (1-alpha)V >=r+B-1,

which is exactly V>=W_r. The zeroth rank is the correct value e-r. Together with Section4 this proves all relative rank budgets and hence the claimed ordinary-target P14 conclusion through the inherited source-target comparison.

## 7. Low-defect corollary: the same gate suffices without d>=5

**Corollary.** With the same actual hitting hypothesis, for every d>=0 and r>=3 the sole condition

    2e+d >= C(2r,r)+r^2-r+1

pays every relative rank. In addition to the inputs above, this corollary uses the inherited universal actual r2 theorem and same-source upward stability.

First observe that the direct argument in Section4 only needs

    (d+1)/2 >= 3-4/(r+1),                                (11)

rather than the coarser d>=5. Thus it directly pays r3 at every d>=3, and pays r4 at every d>=4, under the corresponding harmonic gate. The first-rank argument itself needs only d>0.

If d<=2, A2 has at most two numerical coordinate weights. As E hits each weight, choose an E-variable from a live quadratic factorization of each such weight, and extend the selected set to two variables within E. This actual pair hits all A2 and is paid by the inherited r2 theorem. Upward stability then pays E. This includes d=0, when any actual pair suffices.

If d=3, the same coordinate selection gives a hitting subset of E of size at most three; extend it to an actual hitting triple E0 within E. The threshold function

    H(r)=C(2r,r)+r^2-r+1

is increasing for r>=3, since both summands increase. Hence the r gate implies 2e+d>=H(3)=27. Apply the direct r3,d3 proof justified by (11), then upward stability from E0 to E.

If d=4 and r=3, the direct r3 proof applies. If d=4 and r>=4, choose and extend an E-hitting subset to four elements E0. The r gate implies 2e+d>=H(4)=83. Equation(11) holds at r4,d4 because 5/2>=11/5. Pay E0 by the direct proof, then pass upward to E.

These cases cover every d<5. They use actual E-variable factorizations of each numerical quadratic coordinate, so they do not assume a small hitting set exists merely from a dimension count in an arbitrary quadratic algebra. They prove actual rank payment in the stated gate, rather than converting an inherited small-e P14 theorem into an unsupported small-cut rank assertion.
