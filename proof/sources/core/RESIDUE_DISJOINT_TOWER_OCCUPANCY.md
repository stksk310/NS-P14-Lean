# Same-residue disjointness couples every tower degree, birth, and Tor rank

## Statement and scope

Let `Gamma=<m=g1<...<ge>` be a genuine numerical semigroup, with its
actual minimal atoms, over any field. Put `delta=m-e`, `G=gr(k[Gamma])`,
`x=[m]`, `A=G/xG`, `T=(0:_G x)`. Use the actual opposite-jump tower
of R56 and a square-zero hitting cut `P=k[E]` of `r>=3` independent
non-x forms. These can be actual atom variables or the independent
linear forms already justified in the R56 Green theorem. Write

    U_s=A/F_(s-1),   X_s=(U_s)_(>=s),
    h_(s,t)=dim (X_s)_(s+t),    H_t=sum_(s=2)^nu h_(s,t).

Here `F_(s-1)` removes precisely the non-Apery successor heads with
terminal jump less than `s`, and `nu=reg(Q)` for the numerical Apery
quotient `Q=A/N`. Let `a=dim Q2`, and let `ell` count actual terminals
of order one. Then the following are exact integer statements:

    H_0=delta,
    H_1=delta-a+ell=sum_s mu_P((X_s)_(>=s+1)),              (1)
    sum_(t>=0) H_t=S=sum_(rho!=0)(L_rho-1).               (2)

The new bound is simultaneous over every relative degree:

    0<=H_t<=m-1    for every integer t>=0.                (3)

No separate maximum for each tower module is taken in (3). Each of
the `m-1` nonzero residue classes is used at most once for a fixed
`t`, even when one residue has several x-string terminals with
different jumps. This bound provides a universal source-specific
rank resource. A global P14 conclusion requires its separate target
comparison and assembly; this note makes no such unassembled claim.

## 1. The exact interval attached to an actual coordinate

For a residue `rho mod m`, let `w_rho` be its numerical Apery
element and let

    a_rho(q)=ord_Gamma(w_rho+q*m), q>=0,
    s_rho=a_rho(0).

Because `m` is an atom,

    a_rho(q+1)>=a_rho(q)+1.                              (4)

An actual dead x-arrow occurs precisely when (4) is strict. Its
source `tau=w_rho+q*m` is an actual terminal, of order
`p=a_rho(q)`, and its jump is `d=a_rho(q+1)-a_rho(q)>=2`.
The successor head `tau+m` has standard degree `p+d`.

The numerical Apery coordinate `w_rho`, if `s_rho>=2`, belongs
to `X_s` precisely for `2<=s<=s_rho`. It therefore contributes
one to `H_t` at each integer in the interval

    0<=t<=s_rho-2.                                      (5)

A terminal successor head of degree `p+d` survives in `U_s`
precisely for `s<=d`. Its degree is already at least `s` there.
Consequently its occurrences in the tower are exactly

    2<=s<=d,  t=p+d-s,

so it contributes one to `H_t` at each integer in

    p<=t<=p+d-2.                                        (6)

These descriptions follow from the actual coordinate basis of
`U_s`; they do not split any module filtration and do not identify
the full modules `N` and `T`. All dimensions remain unchanged when
the steering ring is generated by independent linear forms.

## 2. Disjointness within one residue

List the dead x-arrow indices in one fixed residue in increasing
order: `q_1<q_2<...`, with terminal orders `p_i=a_rho(q_i)`
and jumps `d_i`. Applying (4) between their positions gives

    p_(i+1)=a_rho(q_(i+1))
       >=a_rho(q_i+1)+(q_(i+1)-q_i-1)
       >=p_i+d_i.                                      (7)

Hence interval (6) for the ith terminal ends at `p_i+d_i-2`,
strictly before the next such interval starts. Also
`p_1>=s_rho`, whereas (5) ends at `s_rho-2`. Thus every interval
contributed by a single residue is disjoint from every other.

The zero residue contributes nothing: `w_0=0`, and
`ord(q*m)=q` because every atom is at least `m`; thus it has
neither a nonlinear Apery head nor any dead x-arrow. There are
only `m-1` other residues. At a fixed integer `t` each contributes
at most one. This proves (3), over every field.

The argument allows arbitrarily many terminals in one residue. In
particular it does not assume a single finite x-string or a
Buchsbaum tangent cone. Gaps between the intervals are allowed;
no assertion that their union is an initial interval is needed.

## 3. Exact resources at t=0 and t=1

No terminal interval (6) contains zero because a terminal has
positive order `p>=1`. Thus `H0` counts exactly all nonlinear
numerical Apery coordinates, namely `delta`.

At `t=1`, (5) contributes the numerical Apery coordinates of
degree at least three, whose number is `delta-a`. Interval (6)
contains one precisely when `p=1`, since `p>=1,d>=2`. These
contribute exactly `ell`. This proves the first two equalities
in (1). Square-zero hitting makes `M_s=(X_s)_(>=s+1)` generated
in degree `s+1`, so `mu_P(M_s)=h_(s,1)`. This proves the last
equality in (1), with the actual first birth counted once.

Summing (5) and (6) over all degrees gives

    sum_t H_t=sum_(rho!=0)(s_rho-1)
                    +sum_terminal(d-1).

The exact telescoping excess identity in the R56 weighted volume
proof is `sum_terminal in rho(d-1)=L_rho-s_rho`. It proves (2).
The inherited numerical microinvariant bound therefore yields

    S<=delta*V,   V=e+delta/2-3/2.                        (8)

The exact R56 first-birth cap can be retained simultaneously:

    H1<=sigma_cap
       =min(min(r*a,M_2(a))+r*(delta-a), m-a-1, M_2(delta)). (9)

Bounds (3), (8), and (9) constrain the same sequence `H_t`.
They cannot be independently spent as if they came from
different source modules.

## 4. Rankwise transfer as one linear objective

Use the generic ranks `c_j(M)=sum_(i=0)^j(-1)^(j-i)b_i(M)`
and the exact low square-zero baseline

    L_j=e*C(r-1,j)-C(r,j+1)
       =(e-1)*C(r-1,j)-C(r-1,j+1).

The actual Tor long exact sequences in R56 give

    c1(G)<=L1+sum_s c1(X_s)+sum_s mu(M_s),                (10)
    cj(G)<=Lj+sum_s cj(X_s)+sum_s c_(j-1)(M_s), j>=2.    (11)

Every omitted connecting or x-first spectral rank is an actual
nonnegative subtraction. In particular none is assumed to vanish.
Here are the exact signs, so this transfer can be audited without
guessing from Betti-number domination. For an exact sequence
`0->K->M->L->0`, let `d_i` denote the rank of its actual connecting
map `Tor_i(L)->Tor_(i-1)(K)`. Then

    b_i(M)=b_i(K)+b_i(L)-d_i-d_(i+1), d_0=0,
    c_h(M)=c_h(K)+c_h(L)-d_(h+1).

It follows both that `c_h(M)<=c_h(K)+c_h(L)` and that

    c_h(K)<=c_h(M)+c_(h+1)(L),

using `d_(h+1)<=b_(h+1)(L)=c_(h+1)(L)+c_h(L)`.
Apply these to the actual sequences
`0->K_s->M_s->X_(s+1)->0`, and to the actual opposite-jump
filtration of T, whose degree shifts do not change total Tor
ranks. The two-row x-first spectral sequence has only its
actual induced d2 arrows, so its rank identity is

    c_j(G)=c_j(A)+c_(j-1)(T)-rho_(j+1).

Finally use `0->X2->A->A/X2->0`; the last module is
`P/P_+^2 direct_sum k(-1)^(e-r-1)` with exact generic rank Lj.
This proves (10)--(11) and accounts for every connecting rank.
The stronger finite one-degree module theorem from
`../r56/R56_ORIGINAL_PROOFS/combined_audit/HIGHER_HARMONIC_MODULES_AND_GATE_REVIEW.md`
is

    ci(X)<=C(r-1,i)*sum_(t>=0) i/(t+i)*dim X_(b+t),
      1<=i<=r-1,                                       (12)

when `X` is generated in one degree `b`. Its inherited proof uses
homogeneous initial modules, all-field lex consecutive cancellation,
and stable EK. It is not coefficientwise Betti domination alone.

Applying (12) to each actual `X_s` and `M_s`, and then collecting
the same `h_(s,t)` rather than independently maximizing their
generator numbers, gives

    cj(G)<=Lj+sum_(t>=0) W_j(t)*H_t, 1<=j<=r-1,          (13)

where

    W_1(0)=r-1,
    W_1(1)=(r+1)/2,
    W_1(t)=(r-1)/(t+1), t>=2,                           (14)

and, for `2<=j<=r-1`,

    W_j(0)=C(r-1,j),
    W_j(t)= j*C(r-1,j)/(t+j)
             +(j-1)*C(r-1,j-1)/(t+j-2), t>=1.           (15)

For (14), the extra one in `W1(1)` is exactly the actual birth
sum `H1` in (10). For (15), `M_s` starts one degree later:
its relative index is `t-1`, giving denominator `t+j-2`.
These formulas have no extra `mu` resource and no double count.
All `W_j(t)` are positive, and they strictly decrease for `t>=1`.
No comparison between `W_j(0)` and `W_j(1)` is required.
The already accepted universal top-rank theorem handles `j=r`.

## 5. An explicit arbitrary-cutoff corollary

For the first rank choose any integer `D>=2`; for the other
non-top ranks choose any integer `D>=1`. Put

    alpha_j=W_j(D),
    B_j=sum_(t=1)^(D-1)(W_j(t)-alpha_j)>=0.

Since `H0=delta` exactly and `W_j(t)<=alpha_j` for `t>=D`,
(3), (8), and (13) imply

    cj(G)<=Lj+alpha_j*S+(W_j(0)-alpha_j)*delta
                         +B_j*(m-1)
          <=Lj+alpha_j*delta*V+(W_j(0)-alpha_j)*delta
                         +B_j*(m-1).                    (16)

The coefficient `W_j(0)-alpha_j` is allowed to be negative:
its multiplier is the exact value `H0=delta`, not an upper
bound. The substituted `S` coefficient is positive.

In the first rank this simplifies to

    alpha_1=(r-1)/(D+1),
    B_1=1+(r-1)*(H_D-1)-alpha_1*(D-1),                  (17)

where `H_D=sum_(u=1)^D 1/u`. For the higher ranks, let
`C=C(r-1,j)` and `C'=C(r-1,j-1)`. Then the finite sum is

    B_j=j*C*(H_(D+j-1)-H_j)
           +(j-1)*C'*(H_(D+j-3)-H_(j-2))
           -alpha_j*(D-1).                              (18)

For `D=1` the sums are empty and the formula gives zero, with
the standard `H0=0` convention. Retaining (9), or the actual `H1`,
can only improve a valid optimization of the single objective
(13). Formula (16) already suffices to remove the previous
per-layer multiplication of the numerical degree cap by `delta`
or by the independent first-birth bound.

## 6. The same scalar objective pays every relative rank when r>=4

This section independently checks the normalized-weight observation
communicated by the parent branch. For `r>=4`, define the single
actual scalar cost

    F=sum_(t>=0) W_1(t)*H_t.                             (19)

For `2<=j<=r-1`, divide (15) by `C'=C(r-1,j-1)>0`:

    W_j(t)/C'=(r-j)/(t+j)+(j-1)/(t+j-2), t>=1.          (20)

For `j>=3`, both denominators in (20) are at least `t+1`,
so (20) is at most `(r-1)/(t+1)`, which is at most `W1(t)`.
For `j=2`, its difference from `(r-1)/(t+1)` is exactly

    [2-(r-3)*t]/[t*(t+1)*(t+2)].                        (21)

For `t>=2,r>=4`, (21) is nonpositive. At `t=1` it is
`(5-r)/6<=1/6`, whereas `W1(1)` contains the full extra
birth coefficient one. Thus domination also holds at `t=1`.
At zero,

    W_j(0)/C'=(r-j)/j<=r-1=W1(0).

Consequently (13) gives the simultaneous actual rank bound

    cj(G)<=Lj+C(r-1,j-1)*F, 1<=j<=r-1.                  (22)

The same domination is valid for `j=r` if one uses the zero
`cr(X_s)` convention and the `c_(r-1)(M_s)` harmonic bound:
the normalized weight is `(r-1)/(t+r-2)` for `t>=1`, and zero
at t=0; it is at most W1. Alternatively retain the inherited
universal top-rank theorem.

For completeness, the ordinary actual target from the R56
all-rank proof is

    R_j=C(delta+r-1,j)*[e+(j*delta-r)/(j+1)].            (23)

Its exact positive-binomial expansion is

    R_j-Lj=V*[C(delta+r-1,j)-C(r-1,j)]+Gamma_j(delta),

    Gamma_j(delta)
      =delta*C(r-1,j-1)
       +(1/2)*sum_(u=2)^j
           [3*C(r-1,j-u)+(u-2)*C(r,j-u+1)]*C(delta,u)
       +(j-1)/2*C(delta,j+1).                           (24)

Every coefficient is nonnegative. Pascal also gives

    C(delta+r-1,j)-C(r-1,j)
       =sum_(v=1)^delta C(r-2+v,j-1)
       >=delta*C(r-1,j-1).                              (25)

Since `V>=0` on the present actual range, (24)--(25) imply

    R_j-Lj>=delta*(V+1)*C(r-1,j-1), 1<=j<=r.            (26)

Therefore the sole scalar sufficient condition

    F<=delta*(V+1)                                     (27)

pays **every actual relative generic rank of this same cut**
when `r>=4`. Any proved upper bound on the one cost F, such
as (16) or a simultaneous occupancy optimization, can be
substituted into (27). This is a new all-rank sufficient
implication; automatic validity of (27) for a proposed global
parameter range is a separate statement requiring its proof.
At `r=3`, (21) is positive for every t, so this particular
domination argument is deliberately not asserted; retain the
already proved R56 universal triple payment instead.

This derivation uses actual field-specific generic ranks and
nonnegative scalar inequalities. It never asserts equal Tor
ranks in different characteristics. Thus the characteristic
dependence recorded in R56 does not obstruct (22)--(27).

## 7. A further simultaneous prefix resource

The disjointness proof also implies, for every integer `n>=1`,

    sum_(t=0)^(n-1) H_t
       <=sum_(rho!=0) min(n,L_rho-1).                    (28)

Indeed each residue contributes a disjoint set of exactly
`L_rho-1` integer positions in total, so at most n positions
can be in the first n relative degrees. If the shortest
least-offset word lengths are ordered as in the R56 weighted
volume proof, `l_(j)<=j`, and
`L_rho-1<=min(delta,l_rho-1)`. Put `q=min(n,delta)`.
Monotonicity of the minimum and summation give

    sum_(t=0)^(n-1) H_t
       <=sum_(j=1)^(m-1)min(q,j-1)
       =q*(m-1)-q*(q+1)/2.                             (29)

This retains the same-residue resource even across several
relative degrees. It does not assert the pointwise estimate
`H_t<=m-1-t`: terminal intervals can begin far from zero, so
that estimate does not follow from the present argument.
Bound (29) is optional and is not needed to justify (16)
or the scalar/all-rank implication. A consumer that uses it
must optimize the one shared H sequence under all its
constraints, rather than spending each prefix bound separately.

## Dependencies and verification scope

The residue occupancy proof in sections 1--2 is original and
elementary. The actual coordinate description of the jump tower,
the exact weighted-volume identity and bound, the actual tower LES,
and the stronger harmonic module inequality are explicit inherited
R56 dependencies. They are used in their original all-field scope.

`verify_residue_occupancy.py` recomputes numerical orders, all
heads and terminal jumps, the actual tower, and the residue
intervals from raw minimal atom lists. It additionally computes
small direct Koszul ranks in several fields. Those finite checks
are diagnostics of this proof's implementation; they are not a
substitute for the universal residue argument or a claim of
global source coverage.
